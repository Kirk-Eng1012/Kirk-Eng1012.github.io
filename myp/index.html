About

I am an English language acquisition teacher with seven years of ESL/EAL teaching experience, five of them in international school settings.

I teach MYP Language Acquisition (English) and DP English B at Guangzhou Zhongshan Whampoa School for Hong Kong and Macao Students, a fully authorised IB World School. I plan units around MYP concepts and global contexts, teach to each student's phase, and assess against the Language Acquisition criteria.

Before that I spent five years at Future Leadership Academy in Hainan, teaching English to additional-language learners from Grade 1 to Grade 8. From 2022 to 2024 I was also a Homeroom Teacher there, and I co-led the English Department with the school's Foreign Lead.

I hold the Cambridge CELTA and the IB Certificate in Teaching and Learning (PYP), and I am completing a Master of Education at Zhejiang University.

What I believe

Four ideas that shape how I plan a unit, run a class and support a student.

  • Students learn a language by using it to find things out.

    That is why I plan English around inquiry. Reading, writing and talk have a purpose when they serve a question the class cares about.

  • Every student needs a way in.

    A class always holds a range of phases and proficiency. I plan scaffolding and extension in advance, so each student can take part first and then be stretched.

  • Assessment is information for the next lesson.

    Formative checks tell me what to teach next. Summative tasks show students where they stand against clear criteria. I use both to decide what happens afterwards.

  • Students speak up when they feel safe and known.

    Speaking a new language in front of classmates takes courage. I build a class where mistakes are expected, and I get to know each student well enough to judge when to push.

In the MYP classroom

MYP Language Acquisition (English) and DP English B at Guangzhou Zhongshan Whampoa School for Hong Kong and Macao Students, a fully authorised IB World School.

  • Planning units

    I plan inquiry-based, concept-driven units with department colleagues, in line with the IB approaches to teaching (ATT). Each unit is built around key and related concepts, a global context and a statement of inquiry.

  • Teaching by phase

    Students in one class work at different MYP phases. I adapt texts, tasks and scaffolding to each phase, so that every student meets the right level of challenge and can see a next step.

  • Approaches to learning

    I teach approaches to learning (ATL) skills explicitly in each unit, with a focus on communication, research and self-management. Students set goals, reflect on their progress and adjust their strategies.

  • Formative assessment

    Quizzes, exit tickets, oral conferencing, drafts with written feedback, and peer and self-assessment show me how each student is progressing. I adjust the lessons that follow.

  • Summative assessment

    I design and mark summative tasks against the four Language Acquisition criteria, and use the results to report progress and plan next steps.

    • A · Listening
    • B · Reading
    • C · Speaking
    • D · Writing

  • The Language Acquisition outline

    I reviewed and updated the school's Language Acquisition outline so that it reflects the needs and proficiency profile of our students.

Technology integration

How I use AI tools to prepare lessons, to give every student more speaking and writing practice, and to see where each student is.

  • Preparing lessons

    I use AI to draft texts at different levels for different MYP phases, to generate question sets and model answers, and to build interactive materials. I check and edit everything before it reaches students.

  • Speaking practice in class

    Students talk with an AI speaking partner set up for the unit's context. Everyone speaks at once, so each student gets minutes of individual practice that a whole-class discussion cannot give.

  • Speaking and writing practice after class

    Between lessons, students rehearse a talk or draft a piece of writing with AI, get feedback against the task's success criteria and revise. They keep the first draft, so I can see what changed.

  • Diagnosing English level

    I use AI tools to help diagnose each student's English from short speaking and writing samples. Together with my own assessment, this shows me which tasks and which tutorial support each student needs.

Inquiry units

Units I designed on the IB PYP framework for my primary classes at Future Leadership Academy, where I taught from 2021 to 2026.

An inquiry unit up close

Coastal Erosion Protection

  • Grades 4–5
  • 6 weeks
  • Theme: Sharing the Planet
  • Hainan, China

Our school stood beside the sea, so I built this unit around the shoreline and took my students there. I designed it on the PYP framework, with a transdisciplinary theme, a central idea and lines of inquiry.

Central idea

Human actions can both threaten and protect the natural environment.

Lines of inquiry

  • The causes of coastal erosion
  • The impact on local communities
  • Solutions and actions we can take

  1. On the shore

    The unit opened with a field visit to the nearby coast. Students described what they saw in English and came back with their own questions to investigate.

  2. Three ways to show learning

    Students chose how to present their findings: a research report, an interview video or a bilingual poster.

  3. Save Our Coast

    Students organised a “Save Our Coast” campaign and shared what they had learned with the rest of the school and with their parents.

More inquiry units

The Boy Who Cried Wolf

  • Grade 2
  • Key concept: perspective

A textbook fable, reshaped into a small inquiry into truth and trust.

Central idea

Our choices affect the trust others have in us.

Lines of inquiry

  • What trust is
  • How trust is built and broken
  • What happens when trust is lost

In the unitStudents retell the story from the villagers' point of view and from the wolf's, then collect examples from their own lives of trust being built or broken.

ActionThe class writes a trust pledge together.

Rama and Sita

  • Grade 3
  • Key concepts: perspective, connection

A cultural reading taught around Diwali, built into a unit on stories and values.

Central idea

Stories carry the values of the communities that tell them.

Lines of inquiry

  • The story itself
  • The values it expresses
  • How the same story is told across cultures and time

In the unitStudents compare the Indian Ramayana with Thai, Indonesian and Cambodian tellings, working mainly from images, then look for the values carried by stories in their own families.

ActionEach student writes a short story or records an oral story from a grandparent.

Plants

  • Key concept: connection

A plants topic turned from describing plants into investigating relationships.

Central idea

Living things and their environments depend on each other.

Lines of inquiry

  • What plants need
  • How plants and animals depend on each other
  • How humans affect plant environments

In the unitStudents grow bean plants and investigate the trees on the school campus, asking why some are healthier than others.

ActionThe class makes a campus map showing which trees need more care and proposes how to help.
