What I believe

Four ideas that shape how I plan a lesson, run a class and work with a family.

  • Children learn a language by using it to find things out.

    That is why I plan English inside inquiry units. Reading, writing and talk have a purpose when they serve a question the class cares about.

  • Every child needs a way in.

    A class always holds a range of readiness and language proficiency. I plan scaffolding and extension in advance, so each child can take part first and then be stretched.

  • Children learn when they feel safe and known.

    A homeroom teacher is responsible for the whole child: learning, wellbeing, behaviour and a place in the class community.

  • Families are part of the team.

    Regular communication about progress, wellbeing and individual needs lets school and home support the child together.

An inquiry unit up close

Coastal Erosion Protection

  • Grades 4–5
  • 6 weeks
  • Theme: Sharing the Planet
  • Hainan, China

Our school stood beside the sea, so I built this unit around the shoreline and took my students there. I designed it on the PYP framework, with a transdisciplinary theme, a central idea and lines of inquiry.

Central idea

Human actions can both threaten and protect the natural environment.

Lines of inquiry

  • The causes of coastal erosion
  • The impact on local communities
  • Solutions and actions we can take

  1. On the shore

    The unit opened with a field visit to the nearby coast. Students described what they saw in English and came back with their own questions to investigate.

  2. Three ways to show learning

    Students chose how to present their findings: a research report, an interview video or a bilingual poster.

  3. Save Our Coast

    Students organised a “Save Our Coast” campaign and shared what they had learned with the rest of the school and with their parents.

More inquiry units

The Boy Who Cried Wolf

  • Grade 2
  • Key concept: perspective

A textbook fable, reshaped into a small inquiry into truth and trust.

Central idea

Our choices affect the trust others have in us.

Lines of inquiry

  • What trust is
  • How trust is built and broken
  • What happens when trust is lost

In the unitStudents retell the story from the villagers' point of view and from the wolf's, then collect examples from their own lives of trust being built or broken.

ActionThe class writes a trust pledge together.

Rama and Sita

  • Grade 3
  • Key concepts: perspective, connection

A cultural reading taught around Diwali, built into a unit on stories and values.

Central idea

Stories carry the values of the communities that tell them.

Lines of inquiry

  • The story itself
  • The values it expresses
  • How the same story is told across cultures and time

In the unitStudents compare the Indian Ramayana with Thai, Indonesian and Cambodian tellings, working mainly from images, then look for the values carried by stories in their own families.

ActionEach student writes a short story or records an oral story from a grandparent.

Plants

  • Key concept: connection

A plants topic turned from describing plants into investigating relationships.

Central idea

Living things and their environments depend on each other.

Lines of inquiry

  • What plants need
  • How plants and animals depend on each other
  • How humans affect plant environments

In the unitStudents grow bean plants and investigate the trees on the school campus, asking why some are healthier than others.

ActionThe class makes a campus map showing which trees need more care and proposes how to help.

Differentiation
